# Sage: two short website films

Creative draft — 7 October 2026. Prepared from `docs/BUSINESS_MODEL.md`, the pitch and speaker notes, the native app architecture, and the current website brand. This is a production brief and prompt pack, not generated or published video.

## Recommendation

Make a 30-second team film and a 25-second individual-use film. Both follow one specific person through one blocked task. Use Higgsfield for believable human scenes, actual native Sage recordings for the product sequence, and conventional editing for titles, captions, the leaf logo, and timing.

The team film leads because B2B is the chosen strategy. The individual film demonstrates a use case; it does not announce a B2C business model, plan, price, or launch date. No customer results, market validation, revenue, or ROI have been established by these films.

A beautiful ad helps an investor understand the opportunity. A credible native recording establishes that the interaction exists. Paid pilots, measured task outcomes, and renewal evidence must establish the business case separately.

## Product truth

| Show now | Label as planned | Leave out of these ads |
|---|---|---|
| Voice question, current-screen interpretation, spoken guidance, green pointing overlay, user-operated clicks, follow-along on Mac | Guidance grounded in a team's approved procedures | Automatic task execution, guaranteed correctness, claimed time savings, enterprise certification, Windows availability, customer logos, paid traction, settled consumer pricing |

Sage guides the interface today. A future procedure-aware product would also help someone follow an organization's export settings, naming conventions, review steps and handoffs. Its process ingestion and organization isolation are not built. The B2B film makes that next direction explicit in a separate planned-feature end slate rather than showing a fictional live integration.

Individual invite-only tokens and usage limits exist locally. They are not a full self-service account, billing, or team-administration system. None needs to occupy time in a 30-second ad.

## Shared look and sound

- Palette from the website: cream `#F6F1E7`, forest `#1F3B2D`, sage `#8DAA91`, muted gold `#E9B961`. Sage's actual pointer remains `#3DDC97`.
- Type in post: Instrument Serif for the main line; Inter for captions and supporting copy. Use the website's exact leaf asset, not an AI-generated replacement.
- People: capable adults facing ordinary software friction. Expressions are subtle; no helpless stereotype or exaggerated panic. A contemporary Nigerian setting can make the film feel personal without implying customers already exist there.
- Setting: natural window light, warm neutral desks, understated green accents, normal work materials. Avoid a sterile science-fiction office.
- Camera: mostly locked shots and one slow push. A restrained match cut into the screen recording is the signature transition. No orbiting screens, glitch transitions, neon, floating holographic buttons, or constant zooms.
- Audio: quiet original/licensed instrumental bed; one listening cue; voice and click sounds clear. Narrator, user and Sage speak in separate beats. Sage's voice should match the recorded product. Record the founder or a voice actor for other narration; synthetic narration is also an option, not a claim about the product's available voices.
- Captions: useful when muted. Explicitly label the speaker when switching between user and Sage. Keep text short and large enough to read on a phone.

## Film A — “Another quick question” / For your team

**Audience:** owner or team lead at a Mac-using agency, with junior staff and contractors. Initial workflow: preparing a client design for delivery.

**Core tension:** the work is nearly finished, but a routine interface question interrupts someone else. Sage offers help at the point of work. Procedure-aware guidance is the next ambition.

**Runtime:** 30 seconds. Rough timings are editorial targets; rehearse the dialogue before generation. The cast is fictional. “Fieldwork Studio” and its design are fictional production props.

| Time | Picture | Dialogue / audio | Text added in editing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | Junior designer at a Mac turns slightly toward a focused team lead. The lead looks up without annoyance. A finished client flyer is on the computer; screen text is not the focus. | Junior: “Quick question—which export setting?” Brief room sound. | “Another quick question?” |
| 4–7s | Junior turns back to the Mac; tight medium shot. Match cut toward the screen. Use a real keyboard insert only if needed. | Narrator: “Meet Sage.” Listening cue. | “Help, right where work happens.” |
| 7–10s | Actual native screen recording: Canva project, Sage listening/processing state, then pointing. | User: “Sage, how do I export this for print?” | “Native prototype demo · edited for length” in a legible, quiet position. |
| 10–19s | Native footage guides Share → Download → PDF Print. Sage points to the real control; the OS cursor moves separately and the user clicks. Show the download action or resulting file. | Use the actual recorded Sage answer. Target editorial wording: “Open Share. Choose Download, then PDF Print.” This is not a promise of deterministic model output. | Short speaker captions. Never hide the selected setting or pointer. |
| 19–23s | Return to the same people. Junior continues working; lead remains focused. Small exhale, not a theatrical celebration. | Narrator: “Sage points. Your team makes every click.” | “Sage points; you make every click.” |
| 23–27s | Simple cream/forest slate. A small “Planned” tag sits beside a procedure/checklist motif made in editing, not a fake app screen. | Narrator: “Next: guidance built around your team's procedures.” | “PLANNED” / “Your team's procedures. In the flow of work.” |
| 27–30s | Exact leaf logo and website address. No extra scene. | Music resolves. | “Know what to click next.” / “Explore early access for your team” / `sage.kesideonyeocha.com` |

**Optional stronger hook to test:** “The file is ready. The handoff isn’t.” Keep this to a caption variant, not a second plot.

**What this addresses for investors:** a specific buyer/user pair; a repeatable, narrow workflow; current product proof; user control; and an honestly labelled route from interface guidance to procedure-aware guidance. The film does not prove fewer interruptions, fewer errors, ROI or demand. Those are pilot measurements.

**Do not crowd this film with economics.** The $300–750 pilot and $15–25/user subscription are price hypotheses, not settled ad claims. Costs and funding needs belong in the deck or discussion.

## Film B — “The invitation is ready” / For you

**Audience:** an individual Mac user making something outside their normal software expertise. This is a consumer-facing scenario, not a newly agreed consumer business model.

**Core tension:** the creative part is complete, but getting the file out of an unfamiliar tool is confusing. Sage turns a spoken question into visible guidance.

**Runtime:** 25 seconds. Use a fictional community picnic invitation rather than real personal details.

| Time | Picture | Dialogue / audio | Text added in editing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–4s | Adult at a warm home desk studies a completed invitation on a Mac, glances at a browser tab, then back. Mild uncertainty. | Narrator: “The invitation's ready. Getting it out?” A slight pause. | “The idea's ready. The file isn't.” |
| 4–8s | Medium shot, person speaks toward the Mac. Match cut into actual product recording. | User: “Sage, how do I save this as a PNG?” | “Just ask.” |
| 8–18s | Actual native footage: Sage points to Share, Download and PNG. Person uses the OS cursor and clicks. Show the exported file with the same invitation. | Actual Sage audio. Target editorial wording: “Click Share, then Download. Choose PNG.” | “Native prototype demo · edited for length” and brief speaker captions. |
| 18–21s | Same person at same desk, small relieved smile, continues the task. | Narrator: “Sage points. You make every click.” | Same brand promise. |
| 21–25s | Exact leaf on cream; forest title and CTA. | Music resolves. | “Know what to click next.” / “Coming soon for Mac” / “Join the waitlist” |

**Why this is distinct:** the team film sells the prospect of support at work; the individual film sells getting unstuck without leaving the task. A personal invitation makes the latter feel human rather than like another enterprise pitch.

**Alternative personal scenario for a later version:** finding overdue invoices in Excel for a freelancer. It would connect more directly to the current Excel website demo, but it overlaps the business story more. Pick one scenario per film.

## Higgsfield production workflow

1. **Lock the brief and scripts first.** Use these films as two separate projects. Generate actors and settings with Higgsfield's available reference/Elements workflow. Do not assume identical feature names, duration controls or pricing across account tiers/models.
2. **Create reference stills.** For each film: one character reference, one environment reference, and a medium shot defining wardrobe, laptop position and lighting. For the team film, also lock the lead's appearance. Save the chosen assets so the payoff can reuse them.
3. **Generate short human clips.** Make approximately 4–6 second source clips, trimming to the timings above. Use one clear action and one camera direction per shot. Do not pay for a full 30-second render until the character and setting are stable.
4. **Capture the product.** Record native Sage working in real Canva on a clean Mac with fictional assets. Capture at 1080p or higher and include system audio/Sage speech. Record separate runs for PDF Print and PNG. Do not rebuild from the terminal; use the normal Xcode workflow if a native build is needed.
5. **Edit into a deliberate film.** Most of the middle is a full-screen crop of the genuine recording, so compositing a generated laptop display is optional. Add typography, the leaf, captions and a separate future-feature slate in an editor. If compositing, track the display plane and preserve the actual click sequence.
6. **Review one rough cut per audience.** Get the story and legibility right before creating variations. Then export a wide website version and a separately reframed vertical version.

Higgsfield supports references and camera direction, but the model controls the generated pixels. For Sage, precision in the demonstrated pointer/control relationship is the selling point. The recommendation to keep product footage native is our judgment, not a claim that all generators always fail at UI.

## Paste-ready Higgsfield prompts

These describe human source clips. Titles, exact app UI, logo and green cursor are added using native footage and editing. Quoted dialogue in the shot list is a script for recording/lip-sync, not a guarantee that a generation will say it perfectly.

### Shared direction — prepend to each shot prompt

> A photorealistic, understated product commercial for Sage, a Mac voice assistant. Warm cream and muted sage-green production design, soft natural window light, forest-green accents, realistic skin and fabric, contemporary everyday setting. Restrained, premium cinematography: clean natural contrast, subtle texture, believable movement. People are capable and focused; reactions are small and natural. Use the supplied character and location references consistently. Preserve wardrobe, furniture, laptop position and lighting across shots. Keep visible screens soft and incidental; exact software footage and typography will be inserted during editing. No on-screen writing, invented logos, floating interfaces or magical computer actions. One continuous shot, approximately five seconds, framed for a 16:9 master with the person inside the central area for an alternate vertical crop.

### A1 — Team interruption

> Contemporary small creative agency in Lagos, Nigeria. A Nigerian junior designer in their late twenties sits at a light wood desk with a silver laptop. Muted green overshirt over a cream T-shirt. A Nigerian team lead in their thirties sits at an adjacent desk in a charcoal shirt, absorbed in their own laptop. The junior pauses, turns slightly and asks a brief question; the lead looks up with a calm, helpful expression. Do not make either person distressed or incompetent. Medium two-person composition, natural 35mm perspective, camera locked with only a very subtle push toward the junior. Soft daylight from the left, quiet normal working atmosphere. Screen contents remain out of focus. Mouth movement restrained; intelligible dialogue will be recorded or lip-synced separately. No other staff crossing the foreground.

### A2 — Turn back and ask Sage

> Reuse the exact junior designer, outfit, agency desk and left-side daylight from A1. Medium close side angle. They turn back to the laptop, take a small breath and speak one short question toward it while keeping their gaze on the screen. Keep hands resting naturally near the keyboard; do not invent a precise keystroke. Smooth, small push toward the laptop, ending with the near screen edge in frame as an editing transition. Laptop geometry stays unchanged. No floating assistant, no automated mouse movement, no readable generated UI. Natural action over five seconds.

### A3 — Team payoff

> Reuse the two people, exact outfits, desks and lighting from A1. The junior calmly returns to the design task, makes one small natural hand movement on the trackpad, then continues working. The team lead is focused on their own work in the background. Subtle relief, no cheering or high-fives. Medium two-person composition, gentle short sideways drift, realistic depth of field. No newly generated coworkers. Screen text stays unimportant and out of focus. Five-second source clip.

### B1 — Personal task, ordinary friction

> A Nigerian adult in their early thirties, in a comfortable cream knit top, sits at a small home desk with a silver laptop. Contemporary lived-in Lagos apartment: one muted green cushion, a small plant, a plain ceramic mug, warm wood; tidy but not a showroom. Afternoon window light, quiet and welcoming. The person studies the laptop, pauses with a slightly uncertain expression and glances between the screen and a small paper note. The completed invitation is implied by an indistinct warm-colored composition on the display; no readable software text. Medium side shot, natural 50mm perspective, slow minimal push-in. No frustration montage or exaggerated confusion. Five seconds.

### B2 — Ask out loud

> Reuse the exact person, cream knit top, laptop, home desk, plant and afternoon lighting from B1. Medium close shot from a three-quarter angle. They look at the laptop and speak a single short question naturally, as if asking a helpful person nearby. One gentle forward camera movement ending near the laptop display. Hands stay relaxed, face remains consistent, no sudden head movement, no intelligible generated screen text. Intended spoken line for later recording or lip-sync: “Sage, how do I save this as a PNG?” Five-second source clip.

### B3 — Personal payoff

> Reuse the exact person and home setting from B1. After finishing a small task on the laptop, they relax their shoulders and give a subtle satisfied smile, then return to their screen. The invitation task has moved forward; no money, likes, customers or exaggerated success are shown. Medium shot, mostly locked camera, same soft afternoon lighting and warm cream/sage palette. No other person enters. No new props appear. Five-second source clip.

### Correction instructions if a take drifts

Use the failed take plus the locked references where supported, and correct one issue at a time:

> Keep the same face, wardrobe, laptop shape and room layout as the reference. Reduce the expression and body movement. Keep the camera fixed. Remove all floating text and UI. Preserve the soft window light. Only the described head turn should happen.

A separate negative-prompt field is not guaranteed across models; include the constraints in the main prompt when needed. If hand/keyboard interaction looks wrong, cut to a genuine keyboard insert or use a medium shot instead of generating more intricate finger choreography.

## Native capture and edit plan

For each scenario, record enough complete attempts to choose one whose pointer is correct and whose explanation agrees with the interface. Preserve the matched question → answer → target → human click → outcome sequence. Do not splice a pointer from another step onto the wrong instruction.

For the team export, verify that PDF Print is available in the actual Canva account and matches the fictional brief. For the individual export, verify PNG and the completed file. If a setting varies, revise the script to the real interface; do not invent the menu. The transcript depends on model output and should be adapted to the best accurate native take.

Capture at least these states:

- Finished document, before the question.
- Listening and processing state.
- Sage's target next to the named control, before the user click.
- Human click opening the next panel.
- Selected export format and the user's final download action.
- The completed file, matching the document in the first frame.

Allow roughly 12 seconds of readable product action in the team cut and 10 seconds in the individual cut. If the successful native run needs longer, use a 35-second team version or shorten the human opening before speeding up speech. Keep “edited for length” visible if delays were removed; do not advertise the edit as a measured response time.

Use the green overlay from native footage and retain the ordinary OS cursor for the user's click. Sage points; the black OS pointer is what the user operates. Avoid fake “auto-click” rings or an animation that suggests Sage performed the action.

Finalize voice after picture timing is stable. Reserve a clean space for Sage to speak, duck background music, and ensure captions match the words. Use 2–3 second end cards with a readable CTA. An optional caption “Illustrative scenario · Native prototype footage” helps distinguish fictional actors from customers.

## Website placement

Keep the page short: hero/waitlist → one media area → footer. In the media area, offer **For your team · 0:30** and **For you · 0:25** in one player. Default to the team film. Use native clips/captions to replace the existing illustrated demo once the films are ready, rather than stacking two new full-width players above the old one.

Poster ideas: a person and a readable Sage interaction, with “Another quick question?” for the team film and “The idea's ready. The file isn't.” for the personal film. Do not put a tiny complete Mac desktop into the phone poster.

User-initiated playback with visible controls, captions, replay and sound controls. Pause the other clip when switching. No autoplay with sound. Load a poster first and video metadata only; load the selected film when played. Preserve mobile aspect ratio and provide accessible text summaries. Export a wide 1080p master and a separately composed vertical version; a website desktop screenshot is not a phone layout.

The same early-access signup can remain the CTA. The team film can invite team inquiries through a future contact path once agreed; do not invent a booked-pilot funnel or collect sensitive company data in an unimplemented form.

## Review before publishing

Show the draft to a handful of target team leads and individual users. Ask them what problem Sage solves, who clicks, and what they think works today. If they believe procedure ingestion is already live, rewrite the planned-feature slate. If they cannot name the task after one viewing, simplify the opening or screen crop.

Publishing requires finished media and a final native accuracy review. This brief has not generated video, accessed a Higgsfield account, spent credits, changed the website, or deployed anything.
