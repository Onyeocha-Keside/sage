# Sage pitch — speaker notes

Audience: friends who may fund Sage or help sell it.

## 1. Sage

Open with the experience, not the technology. Working prototype, not a claim of product-market fit.

## 2. The problem

Describe a recognizable moment. No quantified training-cost claims have been validated. Ask the audience for examples from their own work.

## 3. Meet Sage

Explain the current native interaction. Leaving clicks to the user preserves control; it does not guarantee correctness or safety.

## 4. See the interaction

These are frames of the scripted website illustration, not native-app evidence. Replace or supplement with a genuine recording before customer or investor outreach.

## 5. The business direction

Company-specific approved procedures are future functionality. Explain this explicitly. The current app interprets screens; it does not yet maintain organizational procedure libraries.

## 6. Our first wedge

Agencies are the initial customer strategy. Qualify Mac usage, recurring workflow pain, buyer access, data approval, and budget. Do not claim interviews already establish demand.

## 7. Why Sage?

Alternatives include HeyClicky, Copilot Vision, Guidy, documentation tools, WalkMe, and Whatfix. We have not benchmarked them or proved superior reliability, deployment speed, or ROI. Small-team scope is the planned entry strategy.

## 8. Founder & progress

Founder claims are limited to work visible in this codebase. Do not add employment history, production credentials, personal motivations, users, or revenue without confirmation. Preserve Clicky attribution.

## 9. Business model

The pilot price is an experiment, not verified willingness to pay. Include model, voice, support, onboarding, and infrastructure costs. Compare against existing written guides and human help.

## 10. What it costs to run

All numbers are estimates from October 2026 list prices and requests measured on the live Worker: about 3,300 input tokens per question (system prompt mostly cached, plus screenshot and transcript) and about 300 output tokens. Claude Sonnet 5.5 is $2 / $10 per million input/output tokens ($0.20 cached). Today speech recognition and the Mac voice are free; production adds streaming speech-to-text (Deepgram, about $0.005–0.008 per minute) and a premium voice (Cartesia or ElevenLabs Flash, about $15–50 per million characters). Each follow-along step is a separate question. The pilot figure is 10 users × 15 questions × 22 days × $0.02. Real usage will differ; the first pilot is the real measurement.

## 11. Path to production

The four upgrades are planned, not built. One-time costs assume the founder builds it; hired development is excluded. Fixed running costs: Apple Developer $99/year, Cloudflare Workers paid plan $5/month once past the free tier, transactional email up to $20/month. Payments via a merchant of record (Paddle or Lemon Squeezy, about 5% + $0.50 per sale); confirm payouts to our bank and country first. The subscription price and margin are hypotheses: a typical user costs about $6.60 a month; heavy users need fair-use caps or pooled team usage.

## 12. The support we need

Friends may help fund or help sell. The $1,000–1,500 figure is the lean one-time plan (≈ $560) plus a buffer for extra pilot usage and surprises. Agree the timeframe, use of funds, and a suitable agreement before accepting money. No instrument is confirmed. Three paying pilots and renewal evidence are targets, not traction.
